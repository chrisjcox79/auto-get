SCP Idea - Security Blanket

24 Dec 2020 15:46

Seeking Greenlights: No

Page Type: SCP Article

Genre (Optional): Drama

Page Layout (Optional): Interview Logs, Incident Reports

Elevator Pitch: A young orphan boy is found in possession of a quilt that creates a play-world which he can escape to as a place of refuge.

Central Narrative: Local reports of a small town indicate that a young boy has been missing for a few weeks after his grandmother passed away. Photographs inside the house reveal nothing out of the ordinary, except for a quilt with thaumaturgical symbols.

A team is sent to the house, whereupon they discover a diary written by the boy's grandmother, detailing that she knitted the quilt, which creates a play-world that the boy can inhabit. She mentions that the quilt's world can be entered with a password, and that she suspects the boy will retreat there upon her passing.

+block

–block

If no one is inhabiting the quilt's play world, its anomalous properties are rendered inert, so SCP decides to attempt to retrieve the boy. First, a D-class is sent into the world as a scout. He discovers a fantasy land divided into four distinct sections. In the center of the world is a large castle surrounded by a city. The world itself is quite safe, with food readily available and the artificial denizens being rather helpful. Each section is guarded by a powerful creature, and upon annoying the boy - who is near-omniscient in the world - he is  by a guardian and expelled from the quilt's world, though he is unharmed when he emerges.

An MTF team is sent in next, which intrigues the boy, as he's never met soldiers before. He encourages the team to play a game with him, wherein they are sent on a quest to kill the dragon of the desert section. As outside weapons are rendered inoperable in the world, they retrieve  by the townsfolk.

The final person to enter the world is a pediatric therapist, who arrives on an almost uninhabited mountain. She dodges the guardian over the next few days, developing a rapport with the boy as he watches her, and secures an audience, where she helps him through his grief. The boy exits the world, SCP secures the quilt, and amnestics are applied. Later, the boy is given a replica of his grandmother's quilt, without anomalous properties.

Hook/Attention-Grabber: The main draw of the story is the whimsy and fantastical nature of the world, contrasted with the boy's mental state. The world reacts to his emotions. It also shows that the Foundation is cold, but not inhuman, and the gentle route in this case is the path of least resistance.

Re: SCP Idea - Security Blanket

28 Dec 2020 00:21

Hello Blackvvwater! lets take a look :)

SAND 

+block

–block

The concept: 

Elevator Pitch: A young orphan boy is found in possession of a quilt with anomalous properties. He is also being stalked by anomalous entities who wish to take the blanket from him.
附加记录：    

My main issue, is there doesn't seem to be much of a connection between the quilt and the entities attached to it. Which as a result, makes the concept feel disjointed and hard to get invested in. Along with this, there isn't much context to why or how this is happening, which makes the skip feel close to a .- why should I as the reader care about the concept? what makes this idea stand out for similar premises? what give this an edge?

Another major issue I have is the blankets anomaly; there doesn't seem to be any limit to what it can do, it can literally get whoever posses it out of any danger whatsoever. As result there's no real conflict or attention grabbing elements, as any possible threats are immediately stopped with no time to really develop.- I would highly recommend you either have the blanket with one or two anomalies or put some sort of limit/restriction on how it operates.

The narrative: 

Central Narrative: An MTF team is sent to a small farmhouse where anomalous events have been reported. During their sweep the team a young boy appears out of nowhere, clutching a quilt with strange designs. The MTF team convince the boy to accompany them back to a site, where the boy is interviewed by SCP personnel.

The boy reveals that his grandmother, who recently died, gave him the quilt, explaining that it would protect him. Study of the quilt reveals that the designs on it are thaumaturgical symbols of protection, and while in the boy's possession, it displays a number of functions. When he hides under it, the boy cannot be perceived. When wrapped around him, the quilt protects him from physical harm. It can also slowly heal wounds. When he ties it around his neck like a cape, it allows the boy to fly. What's more, while the boy can willingly surrender the quilt, if it's forcibly taken, it returns to him in a matter of moments.

During his detainment, the boy also explains that the quilt protects him from  also appear blind to the quilt when it's inert, so it's simply a matter of encouraging the child to surrender the quilt. However, SCP personnel find it difficult to convince him to give up the last thing connecting him to his family.
附加记录：    

Currently it doesn't feel like there's any building tension, nothing is really leading to a payoff or big reveal, which makes the story feel rather anticlimactic. None of the questions that the concept raised are answered here either, as I'm still unsure on what these entities are, why the skip has the blanket to begin with, what the entities motives are and what the the Foundations motives are.

Overall, the base ideas shown in the concept aren't expanded upon much, which makes the story feel anticlimactic. The storyline also feels a bit cliched and flat as it doesn't give much other than .- what will make this narrative interesting? what about it will make the audience invested? who are the characters (this can include the skip itself)? how will they make me as the reader feel?

This is what I'd recommend;

-'Security blankets' are not a new concept, they have likely been done here before, and if they aren't given enough care, they could end up falling into a cliche; you will need to make sure that this puts a unique spin on the premise, try looking through similar concepts in other articles (or even offsite), as they can help with inspiration and comparison, and they allow for you to study how others may have pulled off a similar idea.

- Cut out the entities or give them a strong motive to be after the blanket; currently they feel like a device to create tension as they don't seem connected to the concept, and thy don't play much of a part in the narrative, they are one of the only threats to the blanket. From what I can see, not much (if anything) would need to change if they were removed.

- Nerf the blankets anomalies, and give it more context; this thing is far too powerful, and as a result any possible conflicts are quelled before they can really start. Along with this the blankets origins aren't really given much of an explanation, it doesn't seem to have any real reason to exist, other than to protect the boy (which on its own won't really stand), there needs to be a reason to protect to boy.

That said, if you want any additional feedback (for this or any concept), feel welcome to send a PM. (response times generally range from 2-12 hrs). If your looking for additional feedback, then I would highly recommend the butterfly squad :)

Cheers,

RED

Responding to PM

Hey Blackwater!

It would be a shame if someone

Critiqued it

+block

–block

I can certainly see this as a successful SCP, and the concept/narrative of the blanket being the only thing the boy has connecting him to his family is quite intriguing, and potentially emotional. That being said, there are a few places that your story could be tweaked a bit. A lot of them are similar to Red's, and I'd advising re-reading his post to make sure you improve your concept as much as possible.

Omnipotence:The blanket does seem to have too much power. For one thing, I'm thinking having the blanket be able to make the boy invisible, invincible, and capable of returning to him a bit much. I'd say scrap one or all of these traits, since they don't seem to be adding much to the story.
Mystery: Why are the creatures chasing the blanket? I get the hole mystery of the unknown thing, but I think your story would be a bit more impactful if you gave a reason for why that was happening, and possibly tied it into the backstory of the boy's family
Narrative: The anomalous properties of the SCP are important to your article, but the story contained in it is the most important part. Make sure you focus on the emotions, characters, and themes behind your story.
Greenlights: From what I can see, you're a first time author. If that's true, I'd say  for seeking greenlights, since you need two to get your concept afloat. I'd definitely be willing to do so if you fixed a couple of things up!

Please keep in mind that this critique isn't meant to bash on you or your writing. It's a great core concept, as far as I'm concerned, and I can definitely see it becoming a popular SCP. It just needs a few tweaks and you're good to go! Please do reach out to me and any other butterfly squad members. After all, we're here to help!

So, I was thinking on the security blanket idea, and I'm thinking of going in an alternate idea I had earlier but hadn't fleshed out. What I'm thinking is that the blanket can be activated with a password (like that old game about kids not letting you into the treehouse without a password), and once activated, the user can burrow under the blanket, which takes them into a fantasy world. The world is a relatively small play-world, with a large castle in the center, and protected on four sides by  huge mythical creatures that CAN be killed (the grandmother who designed the blanket wanted her grandson to be able to play hero if he wanted to), but will respawn after about an hour or so. There are a few towns in the world, and the inhabitants are all creepily helpful and friendly. The castle itself can be protected by a number of dangerous traps, making it a perfect source of refuge for the inhabitant.

The underlying narrative still hearkens back to the boy refusing to accept his grandmother's death, but this angle has him turn his play world into a darker source of refuge. The solution still involves coaxing him out rather than taking him by force, as the blanket is impervious to most kinds of harm, since it was also meant to protect. In this way, however, it DOES have weaknesses, namely the password allowing anyone to get in if they know it, and while retrieval is still difficult, it's not impossible. I'm also toying with the play world having rules, such as no guns, though the retrieval teams CAN find weapons inside that work in the play world. They just have to obey the rules the grandmother set up for the blanket.
附加记录：    

Overall, I feel this would still need to be much more fleshed out, considering there still doesn't seem to be any clear goals from the Foundation and the blanket (while having an origin/purpose), doesn't have any clear reason to be protecting this child in particular.

Along with this, I feel this still has too many effects. There's still the invincible blanket, but there's now more effects being added onto that- there's no clear reason reason why these effects are here and the weaknesses are not specified, which (at least to me) makes the skip as a whole feel OP, and close to a .- try to only keep one or two major effects, also try to make sure relate to its purpose.

As for your narrative, there doesn't seem to be any clear end goal, why is the Foundation so interested in this blanket? why are they sending retrieval teams in? how do they find out about the gun rule? what are the other rules? how does everything end?-currently, the narrative pitch just sort of ends?

Another issue I'm noticing is the emotional route you want this to take. Currently, it feels sort of forced as there's nothing really showing the child and how he/she might feel about everything. What will make me invested in them? how do they affect the narrative? what ends up happening to them? how should make me as the reader feel?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

So, first, I'm not sure why you're so opposed to the blanket doing what it's supposed to do - protecting a child. If the SCP took extreme measures, certainly, they could probably find a way to destroy the blanket, but that's not what the SCP does - their mission is to contain first, and destroy only if they have no other option. Besides, there's also the risk that they could harm the child, and it's been stated several times that the SCP is cold, but it isn't cruel. If they can coax the child out instead of simply destroying the blanket and killing the child, then washing their hands of it, I feel they'd take that route. Furthermore, the blanket itself could be quite useful to the SCP, as if something went wrong, it could in fact serve as a line of defense for people who could inhabit it like a bunker. Certainly, if someone finds out about the password, they can enter the world as well, but you DID say you also wanted a weakness to keep it from being too omnipotent. Nevertheless, the SCP could certainly find this blanket useful, in my opinion.
附加记录：    

I don't remember mentioning/recommending the Foundation try to destroy it, which part of the critique(s) gave that impression? apologies in advance if I wasn't clear.

My two major concerns was the blanket having too many effects, and no clear purpose as to why.- (what was the grandmothers reason for making the blanket? why did she feel her child needs this? was he in some sort of danger?)

As for the second major concern; the narrative had no clear arc, and the Foundation didn't seem to have a clear motive, (at least from when it was first described).

That being said, this blanket DOES only have one major effect now - it creates a  for a child to inhabit, while keeping them safe as they're playing. It's just that the narrative has the child turn that playhouse into a refuge, bracing himself for a siege. The narrative would involve a report of teachers noting that one of their students has been missing for days, and when people came to visit, they found the grandmother deceased and the child missing, though the blanket seemed to be giving off a strange glow. Once pictures of the blanket got to (admittedly at this point, disinterested) SCP personnel, they'd recognize the symbols on the blanket as thaumaturgical and investigate it.

This seems much clearer, that said; why was the child's first instinct to run, hide and prepare for siege. What caused the child to feel so threatened/scared?

As it stands;

Once they get a therapist in to help the child get over his grief over his grandmother's passing

This doesn't really sound like something a child would prepare for a war over? I can definitely understand him not wanting to leave and/or creating barriers to stop people from taking him away (especially if he becomes aware people are actively trying to take him away), however off the bat, preparing for an attack doesn't sound like something a child would do (at least not immediately), if there's no imminent threat.

Upon finding the grandmother's diary, it would be revealed how the blanket works, and after some guesswork, they do manage to figure out the boy's password and send a D-class into the world (not yet realizing that the boy is inhabiting the world). There, the D-class would be attacked by one of the Four Guardians and killed. A second person would be sent in, armed with a firearm, whereupon they'd learn that it doesn't work.
附加记录：    

I am skeptical on why the Foundation would decide to send more people in after someone already died, why would they waste resources on something that they already know is actively dangerous? Along with this, trusting a D-class with a gun sounds kinda risky, even if they are 'trustworthy', there's nothing stopping them from firing the weapon off at personnel if they don't want to do something, or feel there's no other option.

quick note: if guns can't work here (presumably for safety), then how/why are people able to die/be killed?- this feels bit contradictory.

This D-class would manage to make it to a town, where the townspeople would helpfully inform them that the  when the grandmother discusses the world in her diary (meant as a record for her own children and grandchildren if they want to replicate her thaumaturgical endeavors), they realize that if they can get the child out of the world, the blanket will be rendered inert for easy storage.
附加记录：    

more of a nitpick: if he was preparing for a siege, wouldn't he also try to protect his people/the town too? why did he leave them out in the open if he felt threatened?

Again, the narrative helps emphasize that helping the kid out IS the path of least resistance in this case. Once they get a therapist in to help the child get over his grief over his grandmother's passing - the reason he retreated into the world in the first place - they manage to coax him out and give him a dose of amnestics before securing the blanket.
附加记录：    

Overall this narrative plan sounds solid to me, the central conflict/emotional investment doesn't feel as forced, and you seem to have a clear idea on what you want to convey. That said, I feel for this to work you will need to show the child and his thought process more, along with the Foundations attempts to coax him out in order to store the skip away. Currently, the child doesn't really have enough of a presence (narrative wise), which makes it hard for me to judge what the draft will look like. As the main 'meat' of the narrative will be focused on the child and how he overcomes his grief.- whats the process behind that?

While she did make it so that guns don't work inside the world, she did create weapons that exist inside the world - a magic sword, a fireball staff, that sort of thing - so that if the child wanted to play hero, he could fight with the Four Guardians and  person is just expelled from the play-world completely unharmed, which would keep with the theme of the blanket gently protecting the child without hurting him. Or, if we wanted to go the more gruesome route, we could say the kid picked up just a touch of thaumaturgy and found a way to disable the safeties in the world. Frankly, thinking about it now, I kind of like the former, as it keeps more in line with the theme of the blanket being a gentle protector.
附加记录：    

My main issue here is the grandmothers motives, there still doesn't seem to be any real reason she made this for him to begin with. There also isn't much on how she may have learnt how to make such an object.

- this doesn't need to be anything super detailed, however without any context, the skip feels unfinished and a little cliched to me, as nothing is really is adding onto the 'security blanket' trope to me.

So, yes, the main narrative would focus on a few people diving into the world to get him out. The child would still be wrapped up in his grief, but deign to address them from his castle, where his voice echoes from the heavens in the world, like the voice of god. He'd first scream at them to get out, then show how scared and lonely he is but still want to be left alone, and then when the therapist comes in, talk out his grief and problems with them as he/she goes along. Each one, though, would have to initially impress him by making their way deeper into his world, RPG style, since it shows their willingness to play with him.
附加记录：    

How do you plan to show this? there isn't much that really gives me an idea on what will actually take place in the narrative, how will this all fit into a draft? what are the child's thoughts on this? how does his relationship with personnel change over time? what is his arc as a character?

So, the grandmother's motives were nothing more than giving her grandson a fun world to play in. Basically, it was meant to be an elaborate treehouse, or video game for him to play around in. She was a student of thaumaturgy - the townspeople think she's a witch. I mean, is there really a need for the world more than that? I think its simplicity even lends it to the narrative. It's the last remnant this kid has of his grandmother's love for her, and her desire to see him happy.
附加记录：    

The child's attitude would slowly change through his dialogue with the personnel that enter the world, particularly with the therapist talking him through it. Short of typing up all the dialogue, there's not really any way I can convey that in a basic outline post. And his arc would be slowly breaking out of his shell, talking to the personnel more, and accepting his loss.
附加记录：    

Aight, I think most of this will be dependant on the narrative execution and dialogue. From what I've seen, this works concept wise. So long as there is some context as to why the blanket was created, (which you have shown).
