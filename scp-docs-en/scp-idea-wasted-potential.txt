SCP idea- wasted potential

02 Jul 2020 19:39

Seeking Greenlights:  Yes

Page Type:  SCP Article

Genre (Optional):  Drama/Emotional

Elevator Pitch:  A man who at night is sometimes visited by alternate versions of himself who all appear successful and happy with their lives (e.g. doctors, business men, members of the military or people in happy relationships.). These apparitions will tell him about things he could have done better and mistakes he made in his life. Telling him that he could have done so much better.

Central Narrative: (edited) The man didn't live a good life before going into containment, drinking unhealthy amounts of alcohol and ending up in a dead end job. The combination of being told he wasn't good enough by himself and being locked in containment by the foundation gave him a distaste for authority, since they both reminded him of the versions of himself and prevented him from trying to improve himself and live a better life outside of containment. The man becomes increasingly angry at his apparitions' mocking and becomes less cooperative with the foundation. One day there is a containment breach at his site and he decides to escape in the confusion to try and have another shot at life and leaves a note as sort of middle finger to the foundation.

Hook/Attention-Grabber:  (edited) I want the reader to feel for the situation of the man, showing his sadness and regret of his choices, then his frustration at those around him and finally the hopeful but unclear ending for him. Hopefully showing the effects of the apparitions on the man and the anxiety that they represent for him.

Additional Notes:  Just thought I'd clarify that the apparitions aren't actually alternate versions of himself, just representations of his anxieties and regrets.

Ok, so I absolutely love this idea at first. A man with a failed life that gets visited by himself every night, stating what he could have done different. This is really engaging, at least for me.

I do think the part he tries to commit suicide is a bit raw in this description as it feels like , which I think you should try to do.

My suggestion for a good ending would be to have his copies not compare his failed life to their successful one anymore after he tried suicide, but yet start to develop a friendship with the SCP, convince him that, even though he made bad choices in his life, he could still turn it around.

Another thing for the narrative: Usual SCP articles are the point of view of the researchers into the SCP's abnormalities, but maybe, you could make this one a diary that is found with the SCP telling the story you told on the narrative together with what I've suggested, and the narrative are the researchers investigating the mental state of the man, and what causes his visions.

[Placeholder from PM]

Hm. I like the premise here, although I'm not sure how popular it will be with the rest of the site. It's perhaps rather on the nose about what it's saying, but I kinda like what it's saying, and I think subtlety can be overrated sometimes.

The problems that he's encountered in his life here (alcoholism, bad job etc.) all feel very generic and impersonal to me. They don't feel specific to a character, at least as you've presented them here, and they feel too general to be making much of a thematic point.

The ending is also something I'm iffy on. Suicide is such a sensitive topic and I'm not sure the inclusion here is justified enough within the narrative. How do you plan to handle that sensitively and realistically?

It's also worth saying that this kind of article is going to be a particularly difficult one to pull off, especially as a new writer. Humanoids are widely considered the hardest to write because of all the work that needs to go into characterisation and believability, and this specific humanoid concept is particularly difficult in that regard. This isn't to put you off, necessarily, but a warning to be careful and to not expect this to be easy.

This is really built around his character so this is the most important element of the narrative to nail down.

Placeholder! Got summoned via PM to take a look-see. Will crit in a bit.

Alright, here’s my take.

+block

–block

At the moment, most of my views on this idea have been expressed quite eloquently by OCuin. The basic premise of your anomaly certainly holds potential, but it’s weighed down by a lack of pay-off at the end of your protagonist’s emotional arc. A convenient containment breach a certain point along this arc doesn’t make up for that.

Allow me to put it another way. If your character gets more and more resentful of authority and grows more and more discontent, you’re building up narrative tension. If your character disappears with an angry note, that doesn’t relieve the tension so much as it lets it seep away, potentially leaving your reader feeling unsatisfied. What ending could you use to provide your character with a clear ending that will resolve the conflict created by previous animosity towards authority?

If you respond to this comment and would like me to see it, please send me a Wikidot PM, as I will not be notified otherwise.
