I really, really enjoyed this. The weird details like the anomaly not affecting people with severed digits was a good start, I honestly enjoyed the description alone enough to upvote this. While I liked the first interview, I was afraid there was going to become a case of too much extra content bringing down the concise and solid main description, but then the escalation of the effect and the story that was told sucked me in.

I love that it gives enough description of the cult to keep us interested without giving everything away. My only quibble is that I feel the Foundation using D-Class from the area where SCP-3873 came from seems like a serious oversight, but you could fix that by making it so the D-Class was recruited from somewhere else in the USA, maybe with a name change, and it wasn't initially apparent he came from Florida. Also, there's a broken collapsible in the addendum area.

Easy upvote and I really hope this gets the attention it deserves because it is a masterwork of an SCP.

Whether you like it or not, history is on our side. We will bury you!

I strongly dislike this.

+block

–block

The subject is Caucasian in descent, possessing blonde hair, blue eyes, and pale skin.
附加记录：    

You call the skip the subject and not the anomaly or the object. (I'm seeing that error more and more these days  is wholly unnecessary when you already specify race.

The whole thing about letting a D-class stay with her was terrifying. I like writing the Foundation as 'the nice side of cold' too but this is too far IMO; the dude's a violent felon. What the hell is the Ethics Committee on in this? The Foundation's job isn't to make sure she's got someone to cuddle with, it's to stop her from hurting people/disrupting normality and keep her safe from herself in the process.

And he ended up hurting her, which just makes their move look dumber from a narrative standpoint regardless of the fact that he did it because he was in the cult. Like, the fact that chances led to him being from the cult just proves that they shouldn't have done it. It's not a good way to introduce the notion of letting someone stay with a skip.

Before security could intervene, Junior Researcher Davis had entered the cell and engaged D-36336 in an attempt to divert his attention from SCP-3873. Agent White discharged his firearm at D-36336, grazing JR Davis's left arm, penetrating D-36336's collarbone, and exiting through the shoulderblade. At which point, D-36336 desisted before being approached, shot again, and killed by Agent White.  SCP-3873 was examined by medical staff and treated for minor blunt force trauma.
附加记录：    

So was Agent White reprimanded for killing a subject who surrendered? Why was he using lethal force in a containment chamber with only one normal human perpetrator anyway? Even civilian tasers can have a range of 25 feet and this room cannot possibly be that big. The Foundation values lives. Even D-class ones, because they need living human bodies to use. Killing someone like this is a waste of personnel. If his behavior is too shit for anything else, he can be amnesticized and assigned to something that doesn't require interacting with a sapient entity.

SCP-3873:  That's it, huh? Just a report? Paperwork? I am a person, doctor! I am a human, just like you!

Dr. Porter pauses.

Dr. Porter:  I know, Riley. We all know. There are many more like you. Many more who continue to suffer fates that we wish we could help them out of. But we can't. That's a reality that most of us have come to accept. The multiverse is a cruel place. I'm sorry. We have to get back to work now.
附加记录：    

The 'I'm sapient waah'/'we don't care I'm doing my job' skip/employee exchange trope is not unique in general or in this application.

It was concluded that the effect had somehow passed onto him when it was neutralized from the initial SCP-3873 host.
附加记录：    

Nothing has been established to indicate that this is a thing that can happen. Dropping it as a surprise at the end just leaves more unanswered questions, and not in a good way.

It has been determined that Dr. Porter contracted the anomaly by sheer chance.
附加记录：    

Yeah.

Locating the current host of the anomaly is a Delta-level priority. I have assigned Mobile Task Force Iota-10 to monitor governmental and judicial chatter to locate the current host and place them into containment. We have a whole world to search. We will not make this mistake again.
附加记录：    

That's cool, but no focus is put on these elements at all until here. The story is about a girl being miserable while men make her miserable, and then the narrative tries to switch to the wide-scale look at the cult's impact on the world instead. The end result is split focus.

Between these nitpicks and some social issue elements I'll refrain from addressing in depth because they're subjective, this whole thing is just uncomfortable and clashes with my headcanon egregiously.

The biggest problem for me is how contrived it is. There's a bunch of really contrived plotting here (the Foundation being willing to leave 3873 and the D-Class together unsupervised for such a long time, the D-Class being a member of the cult, Porter getting the effect by chance), so much so that I initially thought this was one of those articles where someone in the Foundation is removing information to hide something. If that was the intent, and the researchers are covering something up, then it doesn't really coalesce well enough and seems unmotivated. If that wasn't the intent, then this plotting is just extremely contrived and unbelievable.

This article is well written, but still has some issues. Here are some fixes.

Issue: There's no way in hell the foundation is going to leave a D-class alone with a largely defenseless anomaly.

Solution: Add cameras to the cell, have security notice and barge in. Don't even have to change all that much.
附加记录：    

Issue: D-class being a cult member is a very large plot hole, for a few reasons. First off, according to cult, falling in love is taboo. Having him be loyal enough to try and kill her, but unloyal enough to fall in love himself is bad writing. Unless he's a later cult member, she'd probably spot him from meetings.

Solution: have the D-class be fully unrelated to her. Perhaps have them get into an argument, or just have him attack her because he's a violent criminal.
附加记录：    

Issue: Agent whatshisname receiving the curse.

Solution: Have it jump to the surgeon who removed her arms. Simple, obvious, brutal.
附加记录：    

There's some other issues, such as  and the direct jump to the note from DR. French (add an introduction maybe).

+1 to save this joint from deletion. It's got hella potential, you just need to act
