SCP Greenlight Idea

22 Dec 2020 19:00

Seeking Greenlights:  Yes

Page Type:  SCP Article

Genre (Optional):  Horror-ish

Elevator Pitch:  An anomalous, predatory dog crate.

Central Narrative:  A dog crate, which lures in pets and then eats them. By all normal appearances, it is a normal dog crate. But when left alone in a room with an animal, (non-human) it will manifest inside it a toy or some object that the animal will be attracted to. If the animal enters the cage, the door will shut behind it and the crate will eat the pet. Despite being made of metal, the crate still feels pain and reacts to external stimuli. (Largely by slamming the door open and shut, but it can make noises) If placed in a not ideal location for finding prey, the crate can relocate itself by rolling on its side to a new place. The make, model, and ID Number on it correlate to a crate sold by a company, and records show it was sold to a circus infamous for animal cruelty (Revised) (I'm admittedly considering scrapping the idea, but if anyone thinks it has potential, I'll see what I can do.)

Hook/Attention-Grabber:  A non-biological predator that preys on pain, hunts and kills sweet family pets, and has developed a merciless camouflage system.

Re: SCP Greenlight Idea

22 Dec 2020 21:55

This has some similarities to SCP-2128, with that being a bit more interesting due to the historical aspect and the present anomalous properties.

As for you're, I really don't find this interesting. Everything past the cage-like appearance seems cliche: two states, torture, murder, and only moving when not seen. This needs to be developed, mainly to answer the question why? Why does this thing do what it does? This doesn't have to be a full origin story, more along the lines of something to contextualize the anomaly in a way that will either induce more horror or allow the reader to understand something further.

While I find the feeding off of pain aspect somewhat interesting, if this is a predator that eats its prey, why does it go through the trouble of touting the prey, releasing it, then eating it? It feels too convoluted to where I'm now thinking that the other bear form should be cut entirely. Have the crate be the entire anomaly, it draws in prey, tortures them, and eats them. The torturing part does feel slightly overboard, but perhaps that's just me.

As of right now, this will not get a greenlight from me.

This reminds me a lot of SCP-1459 but with less charm to it and more of a focus onjust, cruelty? Idk I don't hugely vibe with the core concept as presented but at the same time, this could be interesting if you y'know, did something with it? Like, a SCP article is a short story: you've got the stuff setting up what the anomaly is but what I really want to know is 'what do you do with the anomaly': how do the Foundation interact with this? What challenges does it raise for them? How do the company that made it and the circus known for animal cruelty tie in here? How are you showing the full horror of this thing instead of just leaving it all implied by the description?
