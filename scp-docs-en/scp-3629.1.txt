I simultaneously hate and love the narrative trick here, which I've decided to call 'elephant in the rooming' because I love to needlessly gerund things. The room (kink spider people) is basically only an excuse to frame the elephant (spider people) as a perfectly normal and accepted part of the universe set up in the article; effectively, it's the narrative evolution of made-up words for things in science fiction/fantasy books. Much like that trick, it has the potential to come off as cheap and hokey in the wrong hands, but I think you manage to just skirt the line of being too sly about it.

It reminds me of some of Jekeled's more experimental work (specifically the Boeing New York disaster) in a pleasant way, and the actual content of the article is plenty entertaining even without the narrative gimmick, so I'm going to upvote.

21 Jul 2018 21:33

Big fan of the concept and the scope here, which is just different for the wiki. More like Law and Order: Special Anomalies Unit.

My main issue is that you're telling a super narrative story, but the last interview just gets so convoluted with fifty different names and characters and interactions to the point where I'm really interested in the crime in question and frustrated that I'm not sure what actually happened with the crime. And in the mix of that, you're dropping all this worldbuilding (the Way of Chalt'tiri, the Rises, the Leaves of Winter) that's coming so quickly that it's distracting from the story that I'm interested in.

I also get the feeling that, sort of like Lord Blackwood or the Catgirls or any other  story, this is intended to be a springboard for a super-extensive canon or parallel storyline. Which isn't intrinsically a problem, but it almost cheapens the importance of the story if I know I'm watching the pilot of a series that's been greenlit for however long you or others feel like writing for it.

I'm still upvoting because it's cool, but there's a part of me that hopes I don't see this referenced anywhere ever again, just so it can be what it is, in its little bubble. I'm also hoping it's rewritten at some point in the future to really streamline that last interview and take it from, like, an 8.1 right now to the 11 that it could be.
