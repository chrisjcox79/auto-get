SCP Idea: {Redacted}

23 Dec 2020 03:49

Seeking Greenlights:  Yes

Page Type:  SCP Article

Elevator Pitch:  This SCP is a phenomenon in which nobody can read any information documented about it. You cannot speak of it, and cannot read of it. Containment procedures (Hints to the entity hidden behind the phenomenon) include an average sized concrete room sealed with two gates and guarded by 3 guards at all times. When entering it's chamber, no less than 4 guards should enter accompanying any other personelle. Cameras are prohibited as they do not work. The actual entity itself (though undescribable) is a large, completely black entity covered in a fur-like substance, which consists of 62% Steel. The entity is very aggressive, and shows the ability to remove information from documents of any kind stored on nearby devices and papers. It is very tough, and can ensure much punishment. People who make eye contact with the entity feel very strong symptoms if anxiety and stress, which wears off a few minutes after.

Central Narrative:  This SCP came into existence when a scientist attempted to document an anomaly that had been discovered by the foundation, only to discover that he could not type the words, he had a very powerful urge not to.

Hook/Attention-Grabber:  This SCP is focused around the idea of 'Wait, it's all Redacted? Well then what is it?' Readers can assume their wildest thoughts after reading containment procedures, which allows the reader some info, put not enough to know exactly what it is. Personelle have come up with a general code when discussing the entity, as experiments showed that it can be referred to by a name. Most people of the Site it's contained at know what it is, (As you can actually see an interact with the anomaly, bit you can't describe it or document it) but the reader doesn't. At the bottom of the article, there could be interviews with people trying to talk about it. I have came up with the idea that there could be a loophole in the phenomenon in which it can be described using something such as morse code or anything similar.

Additional Notes:  All critiques helpful! Something I would like to ask is how would I put this into an article? How do I describe something that cannot be described?

My thoughts  - Note: I am not an experienced reviewer, and cannot give greenlights.

+Show

-Hide

I hope it hasn't been done before.
附加记录：    

Unfortunately, this idea has been done before - notably in SCP-5790. The idea of an anomaly with information being anomalously hard to gain is already called 'antimemetics' - a pretty well-explored concept on the wiki - but that doesn't mean a concept like this wouldn't work. For starters, expand your concept. It's hard to review a story without elaboration on the storyline, as even a bland anomaly can have an interesting storyline. Secondly, see what works in other antimemetic articles. In the articles I gave, its the information they do have, not the information they don't, that makes them interesting - the use of bizarre containment procedures hint at a threat that cant be talked about, and so on. I wouldn't recommend doing these formulas exactly, but try and do something interesting with the concept of a self-redacting anomaly. Finally; if you are gonna go down a comedic route, I just don't think 'so what is it?' will work very well, as the idea has already been used seriously a ton, so it doesn't mesh well, for experienced readers - but that's just my opinion. If you revise this idea, feel free to PM me, and I'll look over it again.

Hope that helps!

hide

The idea now is more developed, which is great to see. The idea that the staff know what the anomaly is but you don't could go in many directions with it. Some questions:

You cannot speak of it, cannot read it, cannot hear of it, and cannot see it.
附加记录：    

You say later on that the staff know what the anomaly is, but can't convey it to you. How do they figure out if you can't perceive it? It could be interesting to see the ingenuity of the foundation staff in this case. Perhaps you could convey the actual anomaly subtly through the article (al la SCP-4428)

This SCP came into existence when a scientist attempted to document an anomaly he had discovered, only to realize he couldn't.
附加记录：    

Did the antimemetic effects come from the interaction between the anomaly and the researcher? Or did were the effects already there? The wording here confuses me.

I would recommend that you tell reviewers what the actual anomaly is under the surface because I think an article would be more interesting if you could figure out what the anomaly is. Obviously, it doesn't have to be obvious, but antimemetic SCP's are often criticised for having no resolution, so I'd love to see what the anomaly is meant to be. Feel free to PM me if you revise the idea.
