[+]Overall

[-]Overall

I like the idea of presenting this as likeiffy?

I think it's because the SCP just, immediately tells us with no real build up or anything; it's just, passive story being told and then ending 'oh yeah i feel in love' and it feels like it needssadness of the angler fish as they make someone they love part of themselves but in turn, slowly lose everything about the person that they fell in love with, y'know? Really play with this in a deeply emotional way.

Part of me wonders if the description could be trimmed down a lot: like, there's some big paragraphs about the mating behaviour of these things and I wonder whether there's a better way of conveying them than dense prose; perhaps just leaving it somewhat implied and only covering it in very broad strokes? Same goes for a lot of the details about -1: I wonder whether there's a way to nail the emotions here without spending so much time setting everything up exactly?

[-]Overall

[-]Line

Gonna note that I can miss stuff; flag things which are right as being wrong and indeed flag things which are deliberately wrong.

I'll likely use green text to indicate stuff to add.

Coastal regions most frequented by SCP-X are to monitored weekly by local marine vessels equipped with sonar capable of bathymetric mapping.
附加记录：    

the 'most frequented' here feels clunky; perhaps just cutting the most would help? It might be that 'SCP-X' is read as a singular thing and so adding 'instances' after to emphasise that there's a whole bunch of them would help?

SCP-X-1 and any other captured instances are to held at Site-66 in a saltwater tank set to mimic ocean
附加记录：    

I'd slap commas are 'and any other capture instances' so this flows better

The tank is to undergo monthly cleaning and upkeep and is to coincide with a feeding schedule consisting of 1200kg of assorted bony fish and crustaceans.
附加记录：    

Not really sure the part about 'monthly cleaning and upkeep' is necessary, and 'to coincide with a feeding schedule' feels awkward here.

Due to SCP-X’s anomalous abilities all maintenance, feedings, and interviews are to be conducted solely by female staff.
附加记录：    

add a comma after abilities

SCP-X is the collective designation assigned to large anomalous instances of Lophiiformes, or the deep sea anglerfish.
附加记录：    

'large' feels vague here; there's an implication this might be meant to be e.g. 'large number of' as opposed to 'large sized' but? Really my concern is just that 'large' and 'anomalous' doesn't actually tell us anything about the SCP.

In addition, all instances possess an exceptionally long illicium or fishing rod, capable of extending at least 100m.
附加记录：    

'fishing rod' doesn't quite clarify what an illicium is enough: I'd suggest cutting that and adding a footnote instead explaining illicium in more words.

In place of the esca or lure, the illicium terminates into a realistic and anatomically accurate body of a human female, connected at the base of the skull.

Have been summoned by PM, so here I come. Pighead In, here's a short review of your draft.

So, this is pretty good when it comes to the writing, but I have two issues with it. The first, the main one, is the fact that for me, the end is really abrupt. The entire thing is pretty short and when I came to the end of the interview, I was like . I felt like there were more things that you could tell, about why SCP XXX-1 wants to find love, about the feelings of isolation it could have (and who were really briefly touched upon on the very last sentence).

And the second thing is that at this moment, for me, this draft is .

What I can suggest to you is to dig deeper into how the mermaids live as a society, what are their collective purpose in life and what is the backstory of SCP-XXXX-1, what are the events who led it to wanting a mate, a person to love, what led it to wanting not just having intercourse, but having a person for spending the rest of its life with. With that, you have the potential to tell a pretty good story about how isolation and being tired of just doing what you have to do can led you to wanting something new in life and experiencing, for the first time, human feelings.

So, think about all that, for the rest, your writing has potential. It's all for me, Pighead Out.

Re: SCP Mermaids of the Midnight Zone

15 Dec 2020 01:34

Was asked to take a look at this. I've got a kind of list of items I want to go over, moving from small to big, if you'll bear with me.

+block

–block

SCP-X instances are believed to operate globally.
附加记录：    

This seems unlikely to me. How does this thing adapt to different sections of the oceans like that?

In addition, all possess an exceptionally long illicium capable of extending at least 100m, and terminating into a realistic and anatomically accurate body of a human female, connected at the base of the skull.

These human lures act as a direct extension of SCP-X, showing them to possess corresponding intellect and the ability to speak.
附加记录：    

Honestly, I'd prefer if the process here were a little more imperfect, like the lure has the general shape of a woman but in daylight it's obviously some kind of growth, or their capacity for speak and intellect is rudimentary at best. It just kinda grounds the idea, I think.

Due to SCP-X’s anomalous abilities, all tank maintenance, feedings, tests, and interviews are to be conducted solely by biologically female staff.

These effects are greatly amplified in biologically male individuals.
附加记录：    

I do not think the phrase  in an SCP has ever resulted in anything good. The fixation on biology isn't particularly necessary in the context of what you're doing, anyways, and adding a compulsive effect to amplify victim's desire to have sex with the fish comes with its own host of problems.

Yes, we were attacked by a madman at knifepoint.
附加记录：    

This whole thing is a significant amount of information pushed into a small space — why not create an addendum separate from the description to convey this in a more natural manner?

Overall it's got the general shape of an article that I'd like —it's short and it's got a little twist of expectations at the end — but I feel like the way you're communicating that twist lacks a level of nuance. Having the fish state their motivations is a bit of an anticlimax. In addition I can't really develop sympathy for the SCP since I get its motivations, but it's still manufacturing victims' desire to breed and overriding their consent. I feel like you're trying to get me to realize that these fish are just lonely but I'm not convinced.
