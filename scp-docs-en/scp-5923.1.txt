There's a lot of things that can be said here, but I think it may suffice to point out first and foremost that a Big Rip scenario is not anomalous. It's a natural consequence of the dark energy equation-of-state parameter being more negative that -1, something that comes out fairly naturally from FLRW equations.

Current best data from the Pantheon sample strongly indicates that w = -1.026, which is within error (not shown here) of w = -1. So while it's unlikely that the big rip will occur, there's nothing in our current understanding of cosmology that precludes this.

There seems to be some reference to , but it's buried in a confusing mess of other thoughts and random diatribes about unrelated things. The fact of the matter is that the cat's sort of out of the bag here, and largely has been since Riess and Perlmutter's discovery of the accelerating expansion of the universe in 1998. It's many people's jobs to discuss the value of w - there's been a Nobel prize assigned for that, and it's fairly critical to an ongoing field of physics.

70% of this is describing non-anomalous science. The other 30% is a tangle of self referential procedures and comments designed to scaffold the fundamentally non-anomalous nature of this article.

I can see the argument that this is too implausible because of how many people are actively looking to track w.

I thought the idea of everyone looking at the data and seeing this trailing thing and then the feedback loop of lots of people freaking out about the drag was cool, and that a cosmic invader simultaneously infohazardouz and baked into 11 billion year-old light, was pretty cool. However, as the above response indicates, too subtle a pay-off for a long trip.

So what's interesting about w? Yes it is within the margin of error. Because white dwarves exploding occurs at a set mass threshold and e=mc^2, we track w = -1, nice clean cosmological constant, using those red-shifts, that was the '99 big discovery. The wobble in w comes from quasars which have huge variance in range, which I understand to be the source of the ?? moment. Big Rip definitely came off as freaky in 2005 when first suggested. w at -1.026 I think implies a rip some e's prior to the projected decay of protons, which is e42-44 years, we're talking a rip sometime deep inside the Degenerate Era. Whereas, you set this thing to -10, we're getting kind of spooky.

Anyways, I respect the downvote with thoughtful critique.

Def. going to tone down the tangents in edits and the plausibility of the containment.

Clearly, this would have done better after going through the sandbox pipeline. :)

Yeah, the issue here is that quasar's aren't fantastic at constraining w right now. Back in 98 we barely had anything more than the Philip's Relationship - MLCS was clunky as heck - for Type Ia cosmology. Riess just barely pulled above the threshold for proving acceleration, he definitely was nowhere near getting w = -1. And that's kind of where quasar cosmology is today. Modern Type II cosmology is also fairly wack, largely because we don't have any sort of SALT or MLCS2K model of standardising brightness.

Point being, I guess, if you want to continue with this, maybe focus more on the , rather than focusing on the non-anomalous outcome of that. And yeah, the containment procedures as-is are fairly implausible.
