Central Narrative: 

The SCP article starts with a picture of a normal pencil with its dimensions shown. This picture acts as a mental image that the rest of the article works to rebuild as the real SCP. The description of the SCP is written in a verbose, but specific way to suggest information about the SCP, such as the pencil being described as a tool used in standardized tests, writing and drawing samples being used to sketch out the space, and physical attributes of the pencil like its length being described as 60’ to represent the 60 minutes the test takes to complete.

The article ends with an addendum interview with two researchers interviewing each other with very specific but almost random questions. The addendum starts with the question “can you tell me about SCP-XXXX” and the response being “No.” The conversation switches almost randomly with several topics that broadly talk about concepts such as what the SCP’s test covers, what happens when the SCP prevents people from talking about the test and how the SCP was discovered. The interviewers will from time to time struggle to answer or say “next topic” in order to show that they are physically unable to talk about certain topics.

Re: SCP-XXXX: ce nest pas un crayon

30 Oct 2020 23:27

Okay, I'm having trouble finding what you're trying to do specifically here. Your summary is talking about a pencil, but you're going into The Department of Miscommunications? I'd like to hear more on the supposed infohazard which occurs whenever writing about The Anomalous Testing Center. More story behind it would be nice, like was the building just discovered one day? Or was it once in use somewhere else, as theres no real indication of that.

Creative representation does work, but you have to get into specifics of how the infohazard works. Does the infohazard not allow you to write about these things, and if so, how does it do this. What I mean by this is how does the SCP itself modify information. For an example, SCP-931 modifies information to be written out as haikus. The same applies here. Give a brief rundown for your critters to understand.

My biggest gripe with this is that theirs no narrative present. Your Central Narrative is effectively a part of your elevator pitch. Your narrative should tell a story of the SCP. For example, how it was discovered, or else how the SCP gained its anomalous properties. This can be done in a multitude of ways (test logs, documents, etc.), but nowadays, readers expect a narrative to be present.

Finally, your hook is rather short, and isn't really a hook at all. Your hook should be what draws your reader in to read further. Make it interesting, and don't make it be something simple, such as  Your reader expects the unexpected, make it unique.
