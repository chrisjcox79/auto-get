SCP Idea: The Necro-Matriarch Broadcast Manifestations

11 Dec 2020 16:30

Seeking Greenlights:  Yes 

Page Type:  SCP Article

Genre:  Drama/Emotional (at least)

Page Layout:  Default SCP article format with four (4) in-page addendums, including the Archived Containment Procedures. All while under the Broken Masquerade highlight

Elevator Pitch: 

A mysterious phenomenon that affects antique radios made during the 1940s era, which allows you to talk to your deceased grandmother/s once again. They will activate only in 3:00 am up to 6:00 am Local Time, and if you're the only active person in the room. They'll instantaneously manifest your favorite food and gives you good chat about your life, while a suiting instrumental classical/swing/jazz music accompanies the scenery. Though, its really unsure whether the one you're talking to is actually the spirit of your dead granny. Also, the grandma that will talk to you depends on how close you were and familiar with them. Nevertheless, the effects of this anomaly is not memetic, making the heartfelt reactions genuine.

+block

–block

Central Narrative: 

Operatives first discovered this anomaly through a post in the Parawatch website (an in-universe website of paranormal enthusiast, in case you didn't know), by a user describing itself as a family man who confidently announces that the antique radio they've bought might be haunted, and also haunting their house. They tracked down this post's location of creation for a possibility of an actual anomaly, and were surprised to find that the location is actually an old abandoned house, but the said radio is still there. What's even more suspicious is a family of the same making once lived there.

After the anomaly is catalog, more similar cases has appeared around the globe, giving the Foundation its first thirteen instances of said anomaly; they then perform their standard protocols. The radio has greatly improved the morale of its site's employees, due to the scheduled interactions they could have with it. After the Veil broke, this anomaly has gone mainstream. Due to the methods of manifestation still being unknown, discovering and selling instances is still rare, though its sale at the anomalous market it belongs to is at least regulated

Hook/Attention-Grabber: 

A wholesome skip idea set in the Broken Masquerade Canon. Basically another hypothetical situation on how the public will react to anomalies; except this time, this anomaly is commercially distributed and also actually beneficial without any side effects, greatly improving Foundation morale as well.

Additional Notes: 

- I've already created a draft of the article beforehand, just ask me if you wanna see it.

- How do I credit another user to be a co-author?

Re: SCP Idea: The Necro-Matriarch Broadcast Manifestations

12 Dec 2020 08:12

Got a PM about this. As a preface, I'm not familiar with Parawatch, so I'll be reviewing this from the perspective of someone for whom the addition of Parawatch doesn't contribute anything immediately positive to the concept. With regards to credit someone as co-author, you would note their name in an author page/on your sandbox draft, and when the piece is mainlisted you would ask a staff member to update the Attribution page to associate your names with the article.

+block

–block

I'll admit that the initial premise of this feels reminiscent of my own SCP-348, even down to the  angle. That said, my main issue about this is that it's kind of too perfect and nice, and also seems to assume that people will always have heartfelt happy reactions to their grandmother being manifested. This portion:

They instantaneously manifest your favorite food and gives you good chat about your life, while a suiting instrumental classical/swing/jazz music accompanies the scenery.
附加记录：    

makes me rather skeptical, since there are plenty of cases where grandmothers have been distant or psychotic or outright abusivedo those grandmothers still give people their favorite food and good chats? What happens if someone loathes their grandmother and just summons her to scream at her? Same goes for backgrounds involving barriers such as, say, language barriers or the case of a grandmother passing away before she ever got to meet her grandchildren. What happens in those cases?

The narrative is also fairly plain and kind of unimpressive do you intend to have any background context as to why this anomaly exists the way it does?

Also, ending note:

Nevertheless, the effects of this anomaly is not memetic, making the heartfelt reactions genuine.
附加记录：    

I'm not sure why anyone would expect the anomaly to be memetic, since there's no inherent transfer of anomalous information involved. I think you may have been thinking of a mind-affecting anomaly? For more information on memetics, see the Understanding Memetics page.

Overall, I feel like for this to work, you're going to need more than just the Broken Masquerade setup to make this  kinda flat story feel more meaningful and memorable. In fact, I myself would be more impressed by the story if the Foundation was dealing with lots of cases where the good intentions of the creator went wrong and pulled a victory via emphasizing the importance of counseling and mental wellness in familial relations or the like. At any rate, have the nice things happen in a way that they need to be earned/maintained, rather than just handed out with few/no problems at all.

Other suggestions:

don't have the Foundation just conveniently find out about the anomaly from a random convenient online post. Maybe have them start investigating when they start fielding reports from people complaining about being angry at/sad about Dead Grandma randomly showing up when they're just trying to listen to the radio.
don't have the Foundation site's morale improve excessivelyeven when the masquerade has been broken, the Foundation shouldn't be encouraging its employees to start relying on anomalies, especially ones they have no control over (for people who develop dangerous dependencies on being able to talk to Dead Grandma, what happens if the broadcasts suddenly stop? Best to not let people become that dependent in the first place)
have the Foundation treat the anomaly with the usual suspicion and skepticism, rather than just welcoming it nigh-immediately and letting people schedule interactions. You can hint at restrictions based on psychological reports and family history being in place in the containment procedures.
