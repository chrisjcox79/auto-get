SCP IN NEED OF GREENLIGHT!!! LIFE GIVING PARASITE

27 Nov 2020 00:26

Seeking Greenlights:  Yes

Page Type:  SCP Article

Elevator Pitch:  The scp is a life giving parasite that infect inanimate objects and gives them vital organs and personalities. These instances usually become suicidal and begin to ponder on why they were given life. All instances that the foundation has encountered have been friendly and interactive. as If they were human. The foundation contains them by putting them in separate decompression tanks. They have monitors on the side that keep track of their vital signs. The foundation also keeps track of the lifespan of the infected instances. The longest living instance lived 5 days. On one occasion, a parasite left the object. That object: (a toothbrush) was able to continue functioning as a regular tooth brush.

Central Narrative:  It is a parasite that gives every day objects the ability to live.

Hook/Attention-Grabber:  The parasites having slow melting acid that they use to make the object its host. Most of these incidents have occurred in households. The origin of the parasites are unknown. There are currently 110 encounters on the foundation record.

I'm not able to greenlight this at all, but my feedback is kind of similar to what Grigori Karpin said. It is hard to tell if there is a central narrative in the SCP. Maybe it is just hard to tell because posts in the Idea Critique form usually don't link the sandbox page. Many series 6 articles are more comparable to short stories than a scientific report like some older SCPs.

Because of this I'll just throw out some random ideas for what you can develop the arc of this article. Maybe you can try following the life span of the mentioned toothbrush that lived for five days. You can show when it first came alive, how it interacted with the foundation, and eventual death. You can try darker themes such as depression and suicide which the inanimate objects thinks about when they come alive. Maybe they're some bad ideas but you get the point.

Overall, I think it's a pretty good and unique idea. You just need a bit more time developing the central arc and it would be a great read.
